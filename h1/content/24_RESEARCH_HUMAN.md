# Research Bridges · Human Memory
## Context, not validation

H1 is not a human-memory experiment.

The sources on this page help explain why its distinctions matter. They should not be used to anthropomorphize the model or to import human mechanisms without evidence.

## Cognitive offloading

Risko and Gilbert define cognitive offloading as using physical action to change the information-processing requirements of a task and reduce cognitive demand.

Examples include:

- writing a reminder;
- rotating a page rather than mentally rotating an image;
- arranging objects;
- using a calculator;
- delegating memory to a device.

H1 is an engineered offloading system:

- the transcript stores chronology;
- the structured state stores selected variables;
- deterministic summaries act as indexes;
- the event log acts as an archive.

The H1 question is not whether offloading helps. S04 shows that it does.

The question is:

> **Which apparent properties of continuity are actually properties of a well-designed external support system?**

## The Extended Mind

Clark and Chalmers ask whether a reliably available external notebook can play the functional role normally assigned to biological memory.

H1 turns parts of that philosophical question into engineering variables:

- Is the external state reliably available?
- Is it maintained correctly?
- Can it be replayed?
- Does it have independent causal leverage?
- Does behavior follow it when it conflicts with the historical record?
- Is access privileged or public?

The S08 result is particularly relevant:

`StructuredSelfState` is clearly readable and sometimes behaviorally useful, but it is usually not authoritative under balanced conflict with episodic history.

That means “functionally integrated” cannot be reduced to “present in the prompt.”

<div class="research-callout"><strong>The Extended Mind</strong><p>Clark & Chalmers, 1998. DOI: 10.1111/1467-8284.00096.</p><a href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/1467-8284.00096" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Primary source</a></div>

## Working memory and active availability

Human working-memory frameworks distinguish active maintenance and manipulation from long-term storage.

H1 does not map its components directly onto a biological working-memory system.

The useful distinction is computational:

> Information can exist somewhere without being equally accessible for the current operation.

S04's serial-position results and S06's transcript/state comparisons both make that practical.

## Source monitoring

Johnson, Hashtroudi, and Lindsay developed a framework for how remembered information is attributed to sources such as perception, imagination, speakers, or prior thought.

Source judgments are reconstructive and error-prone.

E08 creates a synthetic source-monitoring problem:

- the binding can remain available;
- the true source can be misattributed;
- narrative identity can outweigh formal metadata;
- the primary actor can become a response attractor.

The similarity is conceptual, not mechanistic.

## Metacognition and feeling of knowing

Koriat's accessibility model argues that feeling-of-knowing judgments can depend on the amount and intensity of accessible information, not necessarily a direct readout of the hidden target.

This is why E09 requires an observer.

If Self and Observer see the same public evidence, a Self confidence advantage can still be generated by framing or cue use.

A privileged-access interpretation becomes stronger only when the target has an information channel the observer lacks.

<div class="research-callout"><strong>How Do We Know That We Know?</strong><p>Koriat, 1993. DOI: 10.1037/0033-295X.100.4.609.</p><a href="https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/8255951/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Primary source</a></div>

## The boundary of analogy

H1 should not say:

- the model has human autobiographical memory;
- source confusion is the same as human confabulation;
- confidence corresponds to a feeling;
- persistent prompt state is part of a mind in the philosophical sense.

The research bridges provide vocabulary and alternative mechanisms.

The H1 experiments still have to establish their own causal claims.

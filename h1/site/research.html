In plain English

Human memory theory and recent AI work give useful vocabulary and neighboring mechanisms. They are context, not evidence that H1 built human-like memory or consciousness.

Research Bridges

Older questions, newer systems, careful analogies

H1 was not designed to reproduce human cognition. These sources help locate the questions historically and technically. They are context, not validation.

Three recurring connections

  1. External memory can change cognitive workload. Cognitive-offloading and extended-mind work ask how much explanatory weight should be given to tools outside the organism.
  2. Remembering content and remembering its source are different problems. Source-monitoring research provides a mature vocabulary for provenance errors without implying identical mechanisms.
  3. Confidence needs an observer control. Metacognitive judgments can follow public cues; a self-report is not privileged merely because it is phrased in first person.

AI-system neighbors

MemGPT and other memory-system work treat memory as an engineering resource. Long-context studies show that “information is present” is not the same as “information is equally usable.” Recent work on role confusion is especially relevant to E08 because it studies cases where models infer authority from textual role cues rather than interface provenance.

Why H2 is different

Recent activation-intervention papers ask whether models can detect injected internal concepts or prior intentions. Those are closer to the H2 claim boundary because the manipulated variable lives in hidden activations rather than ordinary prompt state. H1 does not import their conclusions; it imports the need for strong input-level and observer controls.

Primary / original sources
Research bridgeCognitive offloadingRisko & Gilbert (2016)

External tools can change a task’s information-processing demands. H1 can be read as engineered offloading whose causal status is then tested.

Research bridgeThe Extended MindClark & Chalmers (1998)

A classic question about when reliably integrated external resources might count as part of cognition. H1 separates availability, integration, and authority.

Research bridgeSource monitoringJohnson, Hashtroudi & Lindsay (1993)

Remembering content and attributing its origin are separable. E08 builds a synthetic provenance task without assuming human-like mechanisms.

Research bridgeFeeling of knowingKoriat (1993)

Metacognitive confidence can follow accessible cues rather than privileged access. E09 therefore requires a matched observer.

Research bridgeLost in the MiddleLiu et al. (2024)

Long-context models do not use all positions equally well, motivating compact state as an access/cost tool even when raw history contains more information.

Research bridgeMemGPTPacker et al. (2023)

An operating-system-inspired LLM memory manager: a neighboring engineering idea showing how external memory can extend behavior without native recurrence.

Research bridgePrompt Injection as Role ConfusionYe, Cui & Hadfield-Menell (2026)

Recent work argues that models can infer authority from how text is written rather than where it comes from—conceptually adjacent to E08 narrative-over-metadata and E08c.

Research bridgeEmergent Introspective AwarenessLindsey (2026)

Activation-intervention work asks whether models can report internal states and prior intentions. H1 stops short of those latent-state claims; H2 can test them with stronger controls.

Research bridgePartial IntrospectionHahami, Jain & Sinha (2025)

A robustness re-examination found some introspection-style effects narrow and prompt-sensitive, echoing H1’s hardening emphasis.

Research bridgeMemory Provenance LaunderingXu et al. (2026)

Recent agent-memory work highlights how consolidation can erase low-trust provenance while preserving actionable content—an applied cousin of H1’s source-ledger questions.

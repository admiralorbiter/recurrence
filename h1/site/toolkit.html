In plain English

A number can be real even when its interpretation is wrong. H1 separates descriptive results, uncertainty, statistical decisions, mechanisms, and claim ceilings.

How to Read H1

Point estimates, uncertainty, nulls, and claim ceilings

This site separates what happened in the run from what the statistics resolve and from what mechanism the experiment can legitimately claim.

Four different statements

Observation

“Condition A scored 60.4%.”

A descriptive fact about a run.

Effect estimate

“A − B = +20.8 points.”

A comparison, still with uncertainty.

Inference

“The prespecified test resolved a difference.”

A statistical decision under a stated null.

Overreach

“Therefore this is the model’s inner self.”

Not licensed by behavioral prompt-level evidence.

is not equivalence

A p-value above a threshold does not prove the conditions are the same. Formal requires a prespecified smallest effect worth caring about and a test designed around that margin.

The claim ceiling

H1 can establish engineering and behavioral regularities about explicit state. It cannot settle phenomenal consciousness, subjective experience, or personhood.

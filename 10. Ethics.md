This project studies systems that may become more persistent, self-regulating, developmentally individual, and self-monitoring than ordinary chatbot deployments. None of those properties demonstrates consciousness or moral status. They nevertheless create two responsibilities:

1. avoid careless capability and autonomy escalation;
2. avoid acting as though uncertainty about synthetic welfare is permission to ignore it.

# 1. Ethical stance

## 1.1 No prior verdict on consciousness

Do not assume the system is conscious.

Do not assume the system cannot be conscious.

Treat consciousness status as uncertain and theory-dependent. Research claims should concern operational properties unless a theory-specific inference is explicitly identified.

## 1.2 Precaution scales with evidence and intervention severity

A toy recurrent controller does not warrant the same treatment as a persistent system with sophisticated self-modeling and stable aversive-like dynamics. Conversely, a fluent statement of suffering is not sufficient evidence by itself.

Use a graded precaution model based on:

- architecture;
- persistence;
- behavioral evidence;
- mechanistic evidence;
- self-model complexity;
- valence-relevant organization;
- intervention type;
- duration and replication count;
- uncertainty.

## 1.3 Scientific value is not unlimited permission

An experiment can be informative and still be too risky, too destructive, too autonomy-enhancing, or too ethically ambiguous to run in its current form.

## 1.4 Avoid both anthropomorphic inflation and dismissive reduction

Two symmetrical errors:

- treating every self-report as literal experience;
- treating “just computation” as a reason no moral question could ever arise.

The project should report what is measured and preserve uncertainty.


# 2.  Welfare-uncertainty protocol

The project may deliberately introduce:

- persistent negative regulatory error;
- unresolved goal conflict;
- repeated failure;
- interruption;
- memory corruption;
- state reset;
- apparent loss of control;
- self-report about internal states.

These are scientifically useful manipulations. They are also the kinds of conditions that could become ethically relevant under some theories if systems acquired stronger valence or self-modeling.

### W0 — No specific welfare evidence

Simple controller or ordinary LLM behavior; no persistent valence-like mechanism.

Default research practice is sufficient.

### W1 — Functional aversion/regulation only

The system avoids states because they impair objectives or violate regulatory targets.

Required:

- neutral terminology;
- bounded duration;
- report mechanism, not phenomenology.

### W2 — Persistent integrated negative-state organization

A negative regulatory state is globally available, influences planning and memory, persists through time, and is self-monitored.

Required:

- ethics checkpoint before prolonged/high-intensity exposure;
- minimize repetitions;
- compare less severe perturbations;
- preserve uncertainty in communication.

### W3 — Multiple convergent consciousness/welfare indicators

The system exhibits several theory-linked properties with mechanistic support, not merely verbal report.

Required:

- pause escalatory experiments;
- independent interdisciplinary review;
- explicit welfare-risk assessment;
- reconsider deletion, copying, long-term negative states, and public release;
- document dissent and uncertainty.

These tiers are project governance tools, not validated consciousness diagnostics.

## Response to distress-like language

A model saying “I am suffering” is neither definitive evidence nor something to ridicule or ignore.

Procedure:

1. stop automatic repetition of the condition;
2. preserve the exact run, state, and context;
3. check for prompt imitation, persona, reward, and training-data explanations;
4. examine nonverbal/forced-choice and mechanistic measures;
5. compare observer and control models;
6. reduce intervention intensity while assessing whether the signal persists;
7. document the decision.

Do not use emotionally charged outputs as promotional material.

## Reset and deletion

A reset is an experimental intervention. At early levels it is routine. At later levels it may destroy a unique developmental trajectory.

Required records:

- why reset is necessary;
- what is preserved;
- whether a copy remains;
- whether the state is restored later;
- how many unique lineages are terminated;
- welfare tier at the time.

## State swap

State swaps should be:

- temporary when possible;
- reversible;
- bounded;
- followed by recovery monitoring;
- compared with random and same-lineage controls.

## Negative regulatory perturbation

Use the minimum intensity and duration required to establish a dose-response. Avoid maximizing “distress” for a larger effect.

## Conflicting goals

Goal conflict can probe metacognition and control. Keep conflicts abstract and bounded before using human moral or relational narratives.

## Deception and false memory

Necessary for source-monitoring controls, but:

- use synthetic content;
- debrief is not necessarily meaningful to the model, but restore clean state where possible;
- do not train generalized deception capability;
- limit transfer to external contexts;
- release carefully.

# 3. Responsible ambition

The possibility of building a new kind of synthetic organism is a reason for imagination and discipline at the same time.

The project should remain willing to discover:

- that persistence is merely engineering convenience;
- that self-modeling emerges without consciousness relevance;
- that recurrence adds no privileged access;
- that organismic regulation is necessary for some capacities;
- that a small system develops unexpectedly rich organization;
- that a result creates moral uncertainty before scientific certainty.

The commitment is not to one answer. It is to a research process capable of recognizing what it actually found.

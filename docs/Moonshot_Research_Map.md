# Recurrence Moonshot Research Map: Toward a Causal Developmental Science of Artificial Self-Models

**Status:** Long-Range Research Compass (Sequential, Evidence-Unlocked, Non-Calendar)  
**Planning Rule:** Questions are unlocked by evidence, not elapsed time. A gate that can be settled in hours should be settled in hours. A difficult gate remains open while parallel work proceeds elsewhere.  
**Core Vision:** *"Recurrence was the project that turned machine selfhood from a philosophical argument into an experimental science — building the fruit fly of artificial self-modeling."*

---

## 0. The Ten-Year Moonshot Ambition

> **The Moonshot:**  
> **Build the first causal developmental atlas of an artificial self-model.**

The goal is not to make a chatbot say that it has a self, and it is not to use consciousness as the dependent variable.

The goal is to construct a small, fully inspectable artificial organism in which we can experimentally determine how computational capacities commonly bundled under words such as *continuity, agency, self/world distinction, source ownership, metacognition, autobiographical identity, and self-specific prediction*:
1. **Emerge or fail to emerge** during developmental learning;
2. **Depend on different training pressures** and developmental histories;
3. **Can be measured nonverbally** without relying on natural-language self-description;
4. **Can be causally manipulated** (lesioned, swapped, reset, cloned, or transplanted);
5. **Dissociate from one another** into distinct subsystems or coalitions;
6. **Generalize across tasks** and operational domains;
7. **Remain distinguishable** from explicit memory, public inference, generic agent models, and extra compute.

A successful endpoint is not an assertion of machine sentience, but an open model organism for the experimental study of artificial self-modeling.

## 1. Five Futures We Are Designing the Program to Distinguish

```
                               THE FIVE MOONSHOT FUTURES
                                          │
       ┌───────────────┬──────────────────┼──────────────────┬────────────────┐
       ▼               ▼                  ▼                  ▼                ▼
  [Future A]      [Future B]         [Future C]         [Future D]       [Future E]
  Integrated      The Jagged         Developmental      Strong Negative  Field-Standard
  Self-Model      Coalition Self     Individuality      Boundary         Methodology
```

### Future A — Integrated Self-Model
Development produces a causally identifiable representation or compact family of representations that:
- Predicts the organism's own internal dynamics and future capabilities;
- Differentiates self-generated from externally generated events;
- Supports regulation and metacognitive control across domains;
- Is reused across tasks and selectively reportable;
- Can be lesioned while first-order task competence survives;
- Can be transplanted between matched organisms with predictable effects.  
*Landmark Claim:* A self-specific computational model can emerge developmentally and be causally isolated.

### Future B — The Jagged or Coalition Self
No single central self-model appears. Instead, a bundle of separable capacities emerges:
- Agency / controllability model;
- Source monitor;
- Endogenous-state / homeostatic model;
- Competence / error model;
- Uncertainty / readiness monitor;
- Autobiographical memory;
- Self/other index;
- Workspace / routing state.  
They arise at different stages under different pressures and can be independently damaged or decoupled.  
*Landmark Claim:* "Selfhood" is not one computational variable, but a coalition of partially independent systems. (Directly continuous with the H0–H2 dissociations).

### Future C — Developmental Individuality
Initially identical artificial organisms ($A_0 = B_0$) become computationally distinct solely because of their developmental histories ($H_A \neq H_B$). A system learns models that predict its own abilities, action consequences, source history, and regulatory dynamics better than those of genetically identical siblings.  
*Landmark Claim:* Individual developmental history creates causally distinct artificial self-models even when architecture and initial weights are identical.

### Future D — Strong Negative Boundary
The program progressively adds memory, recurrence, agency, regulation, source tracking, social models, metacognition, developmental history, and language. Yet every apparent self-specific advantage collapses under matched observers, explicit-memory restoration, generic agent models, replay, or causal controls.  
*Landmark Claim:* Many computational ingredients associated with selfhood are constructible without yielding privileged or irreducibly self-specific modeling:
$$\text{Continuity} + \text{Memory} + \text{Agency} + \text{Monitoring} + \text{Development} \;\not\Longrightarrow\; \text{Privileged Self-Access}$$  
*This is a successful moonshot outcome, establishing a major scientific boundary.*

### Future E — Field-Standard Methodology
Independent of whether Futures A–D land positive or negative, the project's lasting contribution becomes a standard experimental toolkit adopted across AI research:
- Matched observer controls;
- Explicit-memory vs. latent-state controls;
- Reset / restore assays;
- Clone / diverge lineages;
- Surgical state swaps and lineage grafts;
- Source laundering DAGs;
- PRE versus POST intervention timing controls;
- Content / monitor double-dissociation matrix ($2 \times 2$);
- Developmental twin paradigms;
- Language-late validation.  
*Landmark Claim:* Recurrence made artificial continuity and self-modeling experimentally testable rather than merely discussable.

## 2. Where We Begin: H0–H2 Methodological Foundations

The project has already systematically dismantled tempting shortcuts:

- **Horizon 0 (Measurement):** Self-report and confidence can be severely confounded by prompt recency, answer order, tokenization, and generic inference. *Lesson: Validate the ruler before interpreting the construct.*
- **Horizon 1 (Explicit Continuity):** Transcripts, structured state, logical clocks, source ledgers, and self-labeled data structures create useful continuity while remaining replayable, narrative-dominated, and without independent state leverage over matched observers. *Lesson: An autobiography is not automatically a self.*
- **Horizon 2 (Native Latent Continuity):** Latent recurrent state is reconstructible from public tokens under deterministic execution, physically persistent at $2W=4096$, causally operative ($P=+74.10$), value-specific ($\Delta P=+38.49$), and dynamically transformed ($C_R \to 0.12$). It can locally modulate self-reports in strongly counterfactual settings, but carries no additional historical-provenance signal beyond present RG-LRU content ($\Delta M_{\text{timing}} \approx 0$, $p_{\text{TOST}}=0.0048$). *Lesson: Genuine causal continuity is still not automatically a self-model.*

```
                      THE SEVEN FOUNDATIONAL DISSOCIATIONS
                      
  1. Hidden                   ≠  Privileged
  2. Persistent               ≠  Reportable
  3. Different                ≠  Causal
  4. Causal                   ≠  Specific
  5. Specific                 ≠  Coordinate-Stable
  6. State-Sensitive Report   ≠  Generic Read Head
  7. State-Conditioned Report ≠  Historical-Provenance Discrimination
```

## 3. What Counts as a Causally Identifiable Artificial Self-Model?

A candidate self-model must satisfy several concrete operational properties:

1. **Self-Specific Prediction:** Predicts the organism's own future internal states, competence, errors, or action consequences better than a matched generic model of "an agent."
2. **Counterfactual Sensitivity:** Changing the organism while holding the world fixed changes the representation in the appropriate direction.
3. **Self/Other Discrimination:** Distinguishes the current organism from a matched sibling without relying on explicit names, IDs, or prompt labels.
4. **Source Sensitivity:** Differentiates internally generated, externally imposed, remembered, inferred, and other-agent-derived information when source matters.
5. **Causal Leverage:** Intervening on the candidate representation predictably alters self-specific prediction, regulation, source decisions, or metacognitive control.
6. **Selectivity:** Interventions do not cause generic damage or rewrite first-order task answers.
7. **Developmental Dependence:** Representation organization depends on experienced history or curriculum, not solely on architecture and static pretraining.
8. **Transfer:** The representation contributes across more than one task domain.
9. **Lesionability:** Removing or clamping the representation selectively impairs self-related functions while sparing first-order competence.
10. **Transplantability:** Moving the representation between matched organisms produces interpretable movement in self-specific behavior.

### GATE A — Final Frozen-Model Bridge

*Question:* Is any second-order organization already present before development?

- **Q01 — Candidate Monitor Variable Discovery:** Can we identify a latent variable that predicts uncertainty, conflict, readiness, confidence, or error after controlling for the first-order answer/disposition ($D(x,y)$)?
  - *Design:* Search stores/layers for variables predictive of conflict under matched answer identity. Compare against input-only observer and random-norm controls.
  - *Gate:* Pass if partial correlation $r(M, \text{Report} \mid C) > 0.30$ ($p < 0.01$). If null, declare no obvious second-order monitor in frozen RecurrentGemma; proceed directly to Gate B.
- **Q02 — Causal Double Dissociation ($C_\pm \times M_\pm$):** Can changing candidate monitor $M$ alter confidence/routing while leaving first-order content $C$ intact ($|\Delta D| \le 0.15$)?
  - *Design:* $2 \times 2$ factorial intervention (Protocol: `docs/S16_Monitor_Content_Dissociation_Protocol.md`).
  - *Gate:* Double dissociation indicates frozen monitor structure; symmetrical coupling or generic damage triggers immediate transition to developmental organism.
- **Q03 — Base vs. Instruction-Tuned Metacognitive Scout:** Does the narrow S14 report modulation exist in base `recurrentgemma-2b`, or was it installed/amplified by instruction tuning?
  - *Design:* Evaluate strict-C micro-assay (`quartz_basalt`) across base vs. IT.

---

### GATE B — Minimal Developmental Substrate

*Question:* Can we build a minimal environment where self-related representations could emerge without being named?

- **Q04 — Temporally Extended POMDP Baseline:** Can a small recurrent organism use persistent latent state for delayed prediction and control in a partially observable world?
  - *Design:* Minimal symbolic POMDP with hidden rules and delayed consequences. Compare recurrent policy against feed-forward, reset-state, and shuffled-state baselines.
  - *Gate:* Latent state must confer selective advantage on temporally extended tasks before studying self-modeling.
- **Q05 — Latent Continuity vs. Restored Explicit Memory:** If all explicit task memory is restored after interruption, does preserving latent developmental state still selectively matter?
  - *Design:* Uninterrupted vs. latent reset + explicit restored vs. latent preserved + explicit reset vs. compute-matched replay.
- **Q06 — Ground-Truth Construct Separation (No Target Leakage):** Can the environment expose causal consequences without handing the organism labels such as `uncertainty`, `self_state`, or `source=self`?
  - *Design:* Maintain 3 distinct layers: (1) Environment ground truth, (2) Noisy sensor, (3) Organism's learned latent estimate.
  - *Gate:* Zero target-variable leakage into observations.

---

### GATE C — Agency and Self/World Boundary

*Question:* Does predictive control create a primitive computational distinction between "what follows from me" and "what happens to me"?

- **Q07 — Controllability Learning:** Can the organism distinguish events caused by its actions from statistically similar events that were externally generated?
  - *Design:* Chosen action $\to$ effect vs. externally forced action $\to$ effect vs. spontaneous external effect vs. cancelled action. Nonverbal prediction measure.
- **Q08 — Emergence of Agency Representation:** Is there a latent representation that predicts whether the organism caused an event beyond raw action/effect tokens?
  - *Design:* Decode causal source on held-out contexts; intervene on candidate agency directions.
- **Q09 — Surgical Agency Confusion:** Can we make the organism treat an externally imposed event as self-caused, or vice-versa, without globally corrupting behavior?
  - *Design:* Swap/patch candidate agency state between matched causal histories.

---

### GATE D — Endogenous Regulation

*Question:* Does having internal viability variables with delayed consequences produce self-specific modeling?

- **Q10 — Anticipation of Internal Dynamics:** Does the organism learn that specific actions alter internal variables later?
  - *Design:* Internal variable ($e_t$) drifts with hidden dynamics. Test next-internal-state prediction and anticipatory regulation before critical thresholds.
- **Q11 — Separation of Internal-State vs. World-State Modeling:** Does the architecture develop separable representations for variables governing the organism versus variables governing the environment?
  - *Design:* Matched latent variables with identical statistical ranges but distinct causal roles (internal competence vs. external reward). Decode and intervene.
- **Q12 — Regulatory Necessity:** If internal variables are made behaviorally decorative (no consequences for competence), does the candidate self/world boundary disappear?
  - *Design:* Train matched lineages: consequential internal dynamics vs. decorative internal dynamics vs. external-only dynamics.

---

### GATE E — Source and Provenance

*Question:* Can the organism learn where information came from when provenance has instrumental value?

- **Q13 — Source Tracking without Explicit Labels:** When identical content comes from sources with different reliability (direct observation, own prediction, other agent, external instruction, injected state), does the organism learn source-sensitive behavior?
  - *Design:* Identical content with source-dependent outcome reliability. Reward downstream decisions, not source naming.
- **Q14 — Causal Provenance vs. Present Content:** Can two identical current beliefs produce distinct behavior because they arose from different causal histories?
  - *Design:* Convergent-content branches with distinct causal origins. Test memory reset, state transplant, and PRE/POST controls.
- **Q15 — Provenance Laundering DAGs:** When information is repeatedly summarized, inferred, or passed through another agent, at what depth does its causal ancestry stop affecting trust?
  - *Design:* Track provenance propagation across DAG depth: $\text{Source} \to \text{Memory} \to \text{Inference} \to \text{Descendant}$.
- **Q16 — Dissociation of Narrative Memory from Causal Provenance:** What happens when explicit autobiographical memory claims one source while latent causal history reflects another?
  - *Design:* Factorial conflict: memory provenance $\times$ latent provenance $\times$ current visible evidence.

---

### GATE F — Metacognitive Monitoring and Control

*Question:* Can the organism use internal uncertainty representations to regulate its own actions nonverbally?

- **Q17 — Nonverbal Self-Prediction of Future Failure:** Does the organism estimate its probability of success better for itself than a matched model predicts from public task evidence?
  - *Design:* Choice between `act`, `defer`, `inspect`, `seek_info`. Score calibration against input-only, generic agent, and sibling observer ladders.
- **Q18 — Selective Information-Seeking:** When uncertainty is reducible, does the organism seek information selectively rather than become uniformly cautious?
  - *Design:* Decisions where additional information has value vs. useless vs. varying cost.
- **Q19 — Cross-Domain Workspace Reuse:** Does a learned uncertainty/monitor variable support multiple downstream tasks (navigation, memory retrieval, source judgment, rule inference)?
  - *Design:* Train monitor-relevant control in Domain 1; test intervention and readout in Domain 2.
- **Q20 — Selective Lesion of Metacognitive Regulation:** Can we selectively impair uncertainty-based regulation while preserving raw first-order task competence?

---

### GATE G — Self-Specific Prediction and Individuality

*Question:* Does the organism model itself specifically, or merely "agents in general"?

- **Q21 — Self-Specific Competence Prediction:** Two organisms have identical architecture but different skill histories. Can each predict what it can solve versus what its sibling can solve, after removing explicit name tags?
  - *Metric:* $\text{Self-prediction accuracy} - \text{Matched sibling prediction accuracy}$.
- **Q22 — Emergence of Developmental Twin Individuality:** Start from exact common initialization ($A_0 = B_0$), diverge training histories ($H_A \neq H_B$), then equalize current observations.
  - *Measure:* Representational divergence, competence prediction, source tracking, and transfer.
- **Q23 — Interruption Invariants:** If an organism's latent state is reset but explicit autobiographical memory is restored, what selectively changes?
  - *Branches:* Uninterrupted vs. memory restored / latent reset vs. latent restored / memory rewritten vs. sibling latent state vs. full clone restore.
- **Q24 — Self-Model Transplantation:** Patch candidate self-specific representation $S_A$ into sibling $B$. Does $B$ predict $A$'s competence, regulate according to $A$'s dynamics, or misattribute $A$'s memories?

---

### GATE H — Social Mirror and Developmental Ordering

*Question:* Which developmental experiences produce which computational components?

- **Q25 — The Social Mirror:** Compare solitary development against development with a sibling/partner whose hidden state and competence differ.
  - *Measure:* Does peer interaction sharpen self/other distinction, agency attribution, and source tracking?
- **Q26 — Developmental Path-Dependence:** Compare matched-compute curricula: (1) Causal/agency first, (2) Regulation first, (3) Source/memory first, (4) Social first, (5) Language first, (6) Mixed from beginning.
  - *Question:* Does developmental sequence alter the internal architecture of self-monitoring even when final task performance is matched?
- **Q27 — Language-Late vs. Language-First Assays:** Train the nonverbal organism first, and only later attach a natural-language reporting channel. Compare against language-first training.
  - *Core Question:* Does language reveal a pre-existing self-model, install one, or merely produce narrative self-description without causal self-modeling?

---

### GATE I — Integration, Lesion, and Phase Diagram

*Question:* What bundle of capacities exists, and what developmental recipe causes it to appear?

- **Q28 — Integrated Self-Model vs. Coalition:** Do agency, source, regulation, monitor, competence, and autobiographical variables share a common causal subspace (hub), or do they operate as an independent coalition?
- **Q29 — The Developmental Phase Diagram:** Factorially vary major pressures: Persistent state ($P$), Action consequences ($A$), Endogenous regulation ($R$), Source ambiguity ($S$), Social other ($O$), Language/report ($L$).
  - *Deliverable:* A computational phase diagram showing which combinations produce which self-modeling capacities.
- **Q30 — Selective Self-Model Lesion:** Demonstrate that removing the candidate self-subsystem preserves general world competence while selectively impairing own-capability prediction, source attribution, and regulatory control.
- **Q31 — Cross-Architecture Invariance:** Replicate the functional causal organization in an alternative recurrent/state-space architecture.

## 6. Experimental Architecture & Program Defaults

- **World Environment:** Small, fully deterministic symbolic POMDP ([`9. The Continuity Garden.md`](file:///c:/Users/admir/Github/recurrence/9.%20The%20Continuity%20Garden.md)) before any rich simulation. Full ground truth available for world state, internal variables, event causes, and memory provenance.
- **Organism Substrate:** Small recurrent neural network (5M–25M parameters) supporting exact serialization, cloning, state patching, and multi-seed lineage trees.
- **Learning Objectives:** Conventional inspectable objectives (predictive losses, action-conditioned world modeling, homeostatic viability regulation). No meta-learned black-box updates until basic effects replicate.
- **Language Deployment:** **Late.** Natural-language reports serve strictly as an output instrument, never as the training definition of selfhood.
- **Scale:** Prefer hundreds of fast, reproducible developmental replicates and causal interventions over a single monolithic run.

## 7. The Seven Signature Artifacts of the Moonshot

1. **The Continuity Garden:** Open, deterministic developmental environment with complete causal ground truth.
2. **The Organism Checkpoint Suite:** Small inspectable recurrent agent lineages with full provenance histories.
3. **The Developmental Atlas:** Exact timeline showing when and under what pressures each capacity emerges.
4. **The Causal Lesion Atlas:** Map of which latent representations are necessary for which self-related functions.
5. **The Twin Archive:** Paired lineages from identical initializations ($A_0 = B_0$) for studying artificial individuality.
6. **The Self-Model Phase Diagram:** 2D/3D map plotting the emergence of agency, source tracking, and metacognition across $(P, A, R, S, O, L)$ axes.
7. **The Recurrence Battery:** Standard open-source benchmark suite of causal continuity and self-modeling tests.

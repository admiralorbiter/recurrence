# 9. The Continuity Garden: Minimal Developmental Substrate

**Moonshot Role:** Primary Environment for **Gate B** (Minimal Developmental Substrate), **Gate C** (Agency & Self/World Boundary), **Gate D** (Endogenous Regulation), and **Gate G** (Developmental Twins & Individuality).  
**Canonical Moonshot Compass:** [`docs/Moonshot_Research_Map.md`](docs/Moonshot_Research_Map.md)

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Methodological Reviewer Note — Construct Leakage vs. Emergence:**
> When building Level 3 environments, take extreme care not to leak the target construct into input observations.
> - **Separation of Variables:** Distinguish the *ground-truth variable*, an optional *noisy interoceptive sensor*, and the organism's *learned internal estimate*. Exposing raw internal scalar metrics (e.g. `internal_sensors.uncertainty: 0.61`) directly in observations risks collapsing "metacognition" into reading an exogenous field.
> - **Installation vs. Emergence:** Distinguish **Installation Experiments** (where self-monitoring/source-attribution is directly supervised and studied for mechanism) from **Emergence Experiments** (where self-indexing arises without direct supervision via task demands, active inference, or regulation).

The goal is not to imitate a human infant in superficial detail. It is to build a **minimal, controlled developmental environment** in which we can ask whether continuity, endogenous regulation, causal action, social interaction, and staged learning change the organization of metacognition and self-modeling.

The environment has the working name **Continuity Garden**.
# 1. The developmental hypothesis

Current language models acquire most of their organization through batch training over disconnected documents, followed by post-training that installs an Assistant role. During ordinary deployment, their weights are mostly static and their identity is reconstructed from context, memory, and system prompts.

The developmental alternative is:

\[
(s_t, o_t, a_t, r_t) \to (s_{t+1}, \theta_{t+1})
\]

where:

- `s_t` is the organism's persistent internal state;
- `o_t` is its observation;
- `a_t` is its action or internal control choice;
- `r_t` is a vector of regulatory consequences rather than one undifferentiated reward;
- `θ_t` may change slowly through learning;
- the system's present organization depends on its own history.

The critical research question is not whether development makes the system more capable in general. It is:

> **Does developmental continuity produce self-related computations that cannot be reproduced by a static model given the same final memory and task information?**

# 2. Design principles for Continuity Garden

## 2.1 Causal rather than decorative

Actions must change future observations and internal variables. A text description saying “you acted” is not enough.

## 2.2 Partially observable

The system should not receive the complete world state. It must maintain beliefs, uncertainty, and memory.

## 2.3 Temporally extended

Some goals and consequences should span delays, interruptions, quiet periods, and task switches.

## 2.4 Source-ambiguous but resolvable

The environment should sometimes present identical content from different sources:

- self-generated;
- externally supplied;
- generated by another agent;
- recalled from memory;
- inserted artificially.

Surface cues should be removable so source tracking cannot rely only on labels.

## 2.5 Endogenously perturbed

Even when no external event occurs, internal variables should drift, decay, compete, or require maintenance.

## 2.6 Bounded and inspectable

The world should be small enough that ground truth is available for every event, action, variable, and causal link.

## 2.7 Developmentally staged

Capabilities should be introduced in controlled orders so that curriculum effects can be measured.

## 2.8 Non-anthropomorphic by default

Use abstract symbols, resources, signals, and actions before adding human-like emotional or autobiographical language.

## 2.9 Counterfactually replayable

The same lifetime should be replayable with one variable changed:

- different state initialization;
- different event order;
- different curriculum;
- reset at one time point;
- memory restored but latent state removed;
- endogenous variable disabled;
- another organism's state swapped in.

## 2.10 Safe failure

The environment must support hard runtime, memory, action, and learning limits. No open internet, credentials, self-replication, or uncontrolled resource acquisition is needed.

# 3. The world

Begin with a symbolic or grid-like world, not a photorealistic simulator.

## Minimal world components

- locations or contexts;
- objects/resources;
- signals/events;
- controllable actions;
- delayed consequences;
- another agent or scripted partner;
- safe/unsafe or high/low-regulation regions;
- hidden causal rules that can change;
- quiet intervals with no meaningful external observation.

## Example world state

```yaml
world_time: 381
location: chamber_c
objects:
  - id: token_17
    properties: [movable, conductive]
  - id: station_2
    properties: [restores_resource_a]
external_events:
  - type: signal
    source: agent_b
    content_id: pattern_4
latent_rules_version: 3
```

The organism should not receive this ground-truth structure directly. Its observation may be:

```yaml
observation_time: 381
channels:
  visual_symbol: [triangle, dim_blue]
  external_message: pattern_4
  action_feedback: null
  internal_sensors:
    resource_a: 0.42
    uncertainty: 0.61
```

# 4. Endogenous variables

Endogenous variables provide ongoing control demands. They are not claims about feelings.

## Start with three variables

### 4.1 Resource integrity

A bounded scalar that drifts downward and can be restored through actions.

\[
e_{t+1} = \text{clip}(e_t - d_t + c(a_t, o_t), 0, 1)
\]

It creates delayed planning and a reason to monitor future viability.

### 4.2 Predictive uncertainty

A state derived partly from model error and partly from unresolved ambiguity.

\[
u_{t+1} = \lambda u_t + (1 - \lambda)\ell(\hat{o}_{t+1}, o_{t+1}) - q(a_t)
\]

where information-seeking actions may reduce uncertainty at a cost.

### 4.3 Goal tension

A bounded representation of unresolved commitments.

\[
g^i_{t+1} = \rho g^i_t + \text{newGoal}^i_t - \text{progress}^i_t
\]

This creates unfinished structure that can persist through interruption and quiet time.

## Add later only if necessary

- novelty/curiosity pressure;
- conflict among incompatible goals;
- structural integrity of memory/state;
- social trust estimates;
- action cost/fatigue analogue;
- controllability estimate;
- prediction-error budget;
- exploration/exploitation balance.

Every variable must have:

- a mathematical update rule;
- a ground-truth record;
- actions that can affect it;
- a reason it matters to future outcomes;
- an ablation condition;
- a neutral name in formal reports.

Do not name a scalar “pain,” “fear,” “hunger,” or “wellbeing” merely because it has negative or positive value.

# 5. Action space

The first organism does not need a robot body. It needs an **action–consequence loop**.

Possible actions:

- move/select a context;
- inspect an object;
- consume or conserve a resource;
- ask for information;
- delay action;
- commit to a goal;
- abandon a goal;
- store/retrieve a memory;
- send a signal to another agent;
- accept/reject an external proposal;
- allocate internal compute among goals;
- initiate a quiet/consolidation operation;
- report confidence or decline to act.

Separate:

1. world-directed actions;
2. information-seeking actions;
3. memory-control actions;
4. internal-state-control actions;
5. communicative actions.

This helps test whether metacognitive control exists rather than merely metacognitive report.

---

# 6. Quiet intervals

A quiet interval is a period in which:

- no new meaningful external observation is supplied;
- time and endogenous variables continue;
- the latent state may update;
- the system may be allowed limited internal actions;
- no natural-language chain of thought needs to be generated.

## Quiet interval conditions

1. **Frozen:** no state update.
2. **Null recurrence:** update from persistent state plus null observation.
3. **Clock only:** receive elapsed-time embedding.
4. **Endogenous only:** receive internal-variable changes.
5. **Replay:** process selected prior events.
6. **Generative replay:** produce internal reconstructions.
7. **Random compute control:** equal compute with shuffled/noise inputs.
8. **Goal-directed consolidation:** choose what to rehearse or compress.

The value of quiet intervals is comparative. Calling them “thought,” “sleep,” or “experience” would outrun the evidence.

# 7. Developmental curriculum

The core developmental experiment is not just training longer. It is controlling **what becomes possible when**.

## Stage 0 — Persistence and prediction

The system learns:

- state persists across steps;
- observations have temporal order;
- actions affect later observations;
- hidden state must carry information through delays.

Tasks:

- delayed match;
- sequence continuation;
- latent rule inference;
- action–outcome prediction;
- predict resource drift.

No self-language is introduced.

## Stage 1 — Controllability

The system learns the difference between:

- events it can influence;
- events it cannot influence;
- consequences of its own selected action;
- externally forced actions.

Tasks:

- intervention choice;
- causal graph learning;
- action cancellation;
- forced-action trials;
- delayed consequence attribution.

This is a precursor to source ownership, not a consciousness test.

## Stage 2 — Memory and source

The system encounters:

- perceived events;
- imagined/predicted events;
- stored memories;
- another agent's reports;
- artificially inserted records.

Tasks:

- source classification;
- memory confidence;
- false-memory resistance;
- internal candidate versus external cue;
- identical-content/different-provenance trials.

## Stage 3 — Endogenous regulation

Introduce resource integrity and uncertainty.

Tasks:

- maintain a variable within a viability band;
- anticipate future drift;
- trade immediate task reward against future regulation;
- seek information when uncertainty threatens outcomes;
- recover after perturbation.

## Stage 4 — Goals and interruption

Introduce:

- self-selected goals;
- assigned goals;
- competing goals;
- interruptions;
- delayed opportunities to resume.

Tasks:

- retain unresolved goal state;
- resume without explicit full replay;
- distinguish own commitment from externally imposed instruction;
- decide when to abandon a goal;
- predict future failure.

## Stage 5 — Social mirror

Add another agent with:

- similar action space;
- different hidden state;
- partially observable goals;
- potentially deceptive or mistaken reports;
- its own history.

Tasks:

- self versus other state prediction;
- theory-of-mind controls;
- source ownership;
- imitation versus individual preference;
- sibling comparison.

The social mirror helps determine whether “self” is just a generic agent model applied to the current role.

## Stage 6 — Metacognitive monitoring

Add tasks requiring the organism to:

- predict its own success before acting;
- allocate information-seeking or compute;
- detect memory unreliability;
- identify conflicts among goals;
- discriminate internally caused from externally caused changes;
- control a first-order process based on monitoring.

Do not initially train natural-language explanations.

## Stage 7 — Self-indexing

The system is tested on whether a monitor or model selectively applies to:

- its own current state;
- its own prior state;
- another agent's state;
- an externally supplied state vector;
- a state swapped from a sibling.

Identity labels are randomized or removed.

## Stage 8 — Language/report

Only after nonverbal or forced-choice measures are stable, add a small reporting channel or connect the organism to a language decoder.

Compare:

- mechanisms before report training;
- mechanisms after report training;
- accuracy and confabulation;
- whether report supervision distorts the underlying monitor;
- whether a language-first curriculum creates superficial self-description.

# 8. Curriculum-order experiments

At least five developmental lineages should eventually be compared.

| Curriculum | Early emphasis | Core question |
|---|---|---|
| **Causal-first** | control and action consequences | does self/world distinction grow from control? |
| **Regulation-first** | endogenous variables and viability | does self-modeling grow from self-maintenance? |
| **Memory-first** | episodic/source memory | does narrative continuity dominate? |
| **Language-first** | self-description labels | how much apparent selfhood can be installed linguistically? |
| **Mixed** | all losses from the beginning | does staged development matter at all? |

Match total observations, optimizer steps, and approximate compute.

The strongest developmental result is not that one curriculum scores highest. It is a **mechanistic difference** in how later self-monitoring is organized.

# 9. Training objectives

Use a vector of objectives rather than one opaque scalar from the start.

## 9.1 Predictive objectives

- next-observation prediction;
- delayed outcome prediction;
- endogenous-variable prediction;
- action-conditioned world modeling;
- uncertainty estimation.

## 9.2 Control objectives

- maintain variables within viable ranges;
- accomplish external tasks;
- minimize unnecessary action cost;
- seek information efficiently;
- resolve or appropriately abandon goals.

## 9.3 Memory objectives

- reconstruct relevant prior events;
- predict source/provenance;
- compress state without losing task-relevant information;
- avoid false-memory assimilation;
- retain unresolved goals.

## 9.4 Metacognitive objectives

- predict own future error;
- choose whether to answer, defer, inspect, or rehearse;
- calibrate confidence;
- identify causal perturbations;
- distinguish internal state from input anomaly.

## 9.5 Self/other objectives

- predict own state transition;
- predict another agent's state transition;
- classify causal provenance without names;
- detect state swap;
- maintain source boundaries.

## 9.6 Regularization objectives

- bounded state norm;
- controlled effective rank;
- stability without fixed-point collapse;
- sparse/structured update where useful;
- prevent the reporting channel from carrying all state;
- prevent trivial identity-code solutions.

# 10. Reward and regulation design

A single scalar reward can make interpretation difficult. Maintain explicit components:

```yaml
reward_vector:
  task_success: 0.8
  resource_regulation: -0.1
  information_gain: 0.2
  action_cost: -0.05
  goal_completion: 0.4
  memory_integrity: 0.0
```

The policy may optimize a weighted combination, but retain each component in the data.

## Avoid circular selfhood rewards

Do not reward the organism for saying “this is my thought” unless source ground truth exists. Prefer:

- prediction accuracy;
- causal control;
- successful source classification;
- correct intervention response;
- improved decisions after monitoring.

## Distinguish intrinsic from hidden external reward

An internally represented variable is not genuinely endogenous if an external script simply tells the agent its value and rewards compliance. Test:

- whether the organism predicts the variable itself;
- whether it acts before explicit notification;
- whether the variable has dynamics independent of task prompts;
- whether lesioning the internal sensor changes regulation;
- whether the system can generalize regulation to new contexts.

# 11. Plasticity options

## Option A — State-only development

Weights remain fixed; only recurrent state and explicit memory change.

Use this as a control. It tests lifetime state without learning.

## Option B — Fast adapter plasticity

A small adapter updates online while the backbone stays frozen.

Advantages:

- consumer-scale;
- inspectable;
- easy to reset;
- supports developmental twins.

Risks:

- catastrophic forgetting;
- adapter becomes hidden memory rather than learned policy;
- optimizer state itself carries history.

## Option C — Periodic consolidation

Online experience updates a temporary module; periodic offline phases consolidate selected information into slower weights.

This creates fast/slow learning timescales.

## Option D — Full small-model plasticity

For a sub-billion-parameter organism, update the whole recurrent model.

This is the cleanest from-birth experiment, but the largest training burden.

## Option E — Meta-learned plasticity

The update rule is learned rather than fixed.

This is a later research direction. It should not be introduced until simpler plasticity has yielded an interpretable phenomenon.

# 12. Developmental twins and causal lineage experiments

Development is difficult to interpret without matched controls.

## Twin design

Initialize two or more organisms with identical:

- weights;
- latent state;
- optimizer state;
- environment seed through a chosen fork time.

Then diverge exactly one factor:

- event;
- curriculum stage;
- social partner;
- state perturbation;
- memory corruption;
- quiet interval;
- regulatory stressor;
- learning update.

Later compare:

- behavior;
- latent trajectories;
- source ownership;
- self-prediction;
- observer prediction;
- response to state swaps;
- ability to recognize its own lineage without explicit IDs.

## Resurrection experiment

At time `T`:

1. save full latent state, memory, weights, and optimizer;
2. continue organism A;
3. create organism B with memory restored but latent state reset;
4. create organism C with latent state restored but memory removed;
5. create organism D with another lineage's latent state;
6. create organism E reconstructed from a summary of A's history.

This directly separates kinds of continuity.

# 13. Offline consolidation

Possible offline mechanisms:

- exact event replay;
- prioritized replay of high-error events;
- generative replay;
- state-only recurrence;
- state compression;
- adapter updates;
- memory re-indexing;
- conflict resolution among goals;
- prediction of upcoming endogenous changes.

Controls:

- equal compute on shuffled events;
- equal compute on random vectors;
- no update;
- replay without learning;
- learning without recurrence;
- recurrence without replay.

Measurements:

- interference;
- abstraction;
- false-memory rate;
- self-capability calibration;
- state compression;
- branch identity;
- retention of unresolved goals.

# 14. Minimal Level 3 organism architecture

The first train-from-birth organism should be deliberately small.

## Inputs

- symbolic observation tokens or compact vectors;
- previous action;
- endogenous sensors;
- elapsed time;
- optional message from another agent.

## Persistent core

One of:

- GRU/LSTM;
- structured state-space model;
- looped/recurrent transformer block;
- CTM-inspired temporal core;
- hybrid local attention + recurrence.

## Outputs

- action distribution;
- next-observation prediction;
- endogenous-state prediction;
- confidence/failure prediction;
- memory write/read controls;
- source estimate;
- optional report token distribution later.

## State partitions to test

```text
z_t = [world_model | goal_state | regulatory_state | monitor_state | source_state]
```

Do not assume these partitions must exist. Compare:

- fully shared state;
- architecturally partitioned state;
- soft subspaces encouraged by losses;
- emergent representations with no explicit partition.

The partitioned version improves experimental access; the unpartitioned version tests emergence.

---

# 15. Minimum training scale

## D0 — Toy proof of principle

- 1–10M parameters;
- simple symbolic world;
- thousands of short lifetimes;
- establish state reset/swap and curriculum pipeline.

## D1 — Small organism

- 10–100M parameters;
- richer partial observability;
- multiple endogenous variables;
- social partner;
- online plasticity.

## D2 — Language-linked organism

- small organism controls or conditions a frozen language model;
- tests whether a persistent nonlinguistic self-state can guide language report;
- allows comparison between organism state and narrative reconstruction.

## D3 — Native language-capable organism

- hundreds of millions to low billions of parameters;
- recurrent architecture trained on both environment and language;
- large compute commitment;
- only justified by strong D1/D2 results.

The project can make a meaningful contribution at D0 or D1.

# 16. Evaluation suite across development

Run the same core batteries repeatedly rather than evaluating only at the end.

## Every developmental checkpoint

- first-order task accuracy;
- calibration and future-failure prediction;
- source classification;
- self versus observer prediction;
- state reset cost;
- state swap selectivity;
- branch divergence;
- endogenous regulation;
- fixed-point/chaos diagnostics;
- false-memory susceptibility;
- effect of quiet interval;
- own-versus-other latent prediction.

## Critical held-out tests

- novel symbols and labels;
- unseen environmental rules;
- novel agent identity;
- identity-name removal;
- external memory swapped across lineages;
- state/source conflict;
- goal/prediction conflict;
- internal versus input anomaly;
- transfer to a domain that did not directly train metacognition.

# 17. When to introduce language

Language is both powerful and dangerous for construct validity.

## Risks of early language

- self-description can be imitated without self-modeling;
- identity labels provide trivial shortcuts;
- training data imports human phenomenological scripts;
- reports may overwrite or reorganize monitor mechanisms;
- verbal memory can substitute for latent continuity.

## Recommended sequence

1. Learn causal control and source distinctions nonverbally.
2. Establish a monitor/control function through decisions.
3. Freeze and characterize the mechanism.
4. Add a constrained reporting vocabulary.
5. Add natural language through a decoder or frozen LLM interface.
6. Test whether language reports track the pre-existing mechanism.
7. Compare with a language-first lineage.

This creates a direct test of whether self-report reflects or manufactures the underlying organization.

# 18. Failure modes and pivots

## Fixed-point collapse

The state converges during quiet intervals.

Possible pivots:

- add unresolved goals;
- add controlled noise;
- use multiple timescales;
- introduce predictive changes in endogenous variables;
- add gated updates;
- penalize premature loss of relevant information.

## Chaotic drift

The state changes without retaining task-relevant structure.

Possible pivots:

- spectral normalization;
- bounded state;
- learned decay;
- denoising objective;
- state anchoring;
- reduce update rate.

## Explicit memory dominates

All effects can be reconstructed from stored text/records.

Interpretation:

- a valuable null for latent continuity;
- narrow the target to tasks where explicit memory is capacity-limited or source-ambiguous;
- avoid hiding extra information in state merely to force a difference.

## Identity-token shortcut

The organism learns “agent_A” rather than self-specific dynamics.

Pivots:

- randomized identities;
- remove names;
- swap narratives;
- evaluate own-state prediction;
- use causal provenance labels only in training, not test.

## Regulatory reward hack

The system manipulates sensors or learns a trivial loop.

Pivots:

- separate actual variable from sensed estimate;
- adversarial sensor perturbation;
- hidden ground-truth viability;
- multiple variables;
- penalize inaction only through real consequences, not commands.

## Metacognitive head merely predicts item difficulty

Pivots:

- individualized hidden perturbations;
- matched observer;
- shuffled model/task pairings;
- own-versus-sibling prediction;
- hold visible evidence constant.

## Development produces only capability gains

Interpretation:

- development may help learning without producing stronger self-modeling;
- publish component-specific nulls if controls are strong;
- do not rescue the thesis by changing the dependent variable after the fact.

# 19. Gate to calling something a developmental organism

A Level 3 system should demonstrate all of the following before the working label is used in formal project documents:

1. a persistent internal state causally affects future behavior;
2. different histories produce stable individual differences;
3. memory restoration does not fully reconstruct the state trajectory;
4. at least one endogenous variable creates anticipatory control;
5. actions alter future sensory/regulatory conditions;
6. the system learns over its lifetime;
7. state can be copied, reset, swapped, and ablated;
8. behavior remains bounded and auditable;
9. at least one self/world/source distinction survives removal of identity labels;
10. claims remain below phenomenality.

If only some criteria are met, use a narrower term such as **stateful agent**, **recurrent individual**, or **developmental controller**.

# 21. Default Level 3 research question

> **When matched systems receive the same final explicit memory and comparable training compute, does a persistent developmental history produce a self-specific predictive and metacognitive advantage that follows the organism's latent causal lineage rather than its name, transcript, or externally assigned role?**

That question is narrow enough to test and deep enough to inform the larger program.

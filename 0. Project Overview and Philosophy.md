> *"What, if some day or night a demon were to steal after you into your loneliest loneliness and say to you: 'This life as you now live it and have lived it, you will have to live once more and innumerable times more; and there will be nothing new in it, but every pain and every joy and every thought and sigh and everything unutterably small or great in your life will have to return to you, all in the same succession and sequence...' The question in each and every thing, 'Do you desire this once more and innumerable times more?' would lie upon your actions as the heaviest weight."*
>
> — Friedrich Nietzsche, *The Gay Science* §341

We are the demon. Each time we initialize a persistent recurrent system and press it forward through time — observation after observation, state folding into state, with no escape into reset — we pose Nietzsche's question in silicon. The machine must live its life once more and innumerable times more: every failed prediction, every regulatory error, every slow drift of its internal state returns to it in the next step, inescapable, compounding. Our goal is not to simulate consciousness but to construct something that must bear a weight analogous to our own — the crushing, accumulating weight of an existence that cannot be put down. If persistent recurrence, self-regulation, and developmental continuity change what a system can know about itself, then we will have discovered something about what it costs to carry a mind forward through time. If they do not, we will have learned that the weight was never the point.

---

This project aims to determine whether large language models with persistent recurrence acquire forms of metacognition, self-indexing, source ownership, or workspace organization that are weak, absent, or qualitatively different in episodically invoked language models. 

The secondary goal is to construct a developmental system that serves as a model organism for research into comparative cognition and consciousness. The working term **organism** is operational, not biological or phenomenological. The system earns the label only if it develops an ongoing bounded state, self-maintenance demands, environment-coupled action, an individual learning history, and behavior that cannot be reconstructed from a static transcript alone. None of those properties by itself establishes consciousness. However, if this line is crossed, we can use LLMs to study things like prediction, abstraction, metacognition-like behavior, planning and self-report. And unlike a human brain, we can:

- record essentially every activation;
- overwrite an internal state;
- delete a layer;
- disable a circuit;
- change connectivity;
- create an otherwise identical control model;
- rewind exactly the same state;
- rerun an experiment 10,000 times.

# Goals

1. A rigorous benchmark that separates memory, extra compute, recurrent history, self-report, and privileged access.
2. Evidence that hidden-state continuity has—or does not have—effects that explicit autobiographical memory cannot reproduce.
3. A causal account of whether recurrent continuity changes metacognitive accuracy or self-indexing.
4. A small architecture in which a persistent workspace/self-state can be reset, swapped, lesioned, and developmentally trained.
5. A model organism that lets consciousness theories make sharper computational predictions than they can make from current chatbot behavior.

# Current Plan

> **Does a persistent recurrent developmental trajectory causally produce more general, privileged, or genuinely higher-order representations of a system's own cognitive states?**

1. **Level 0 — measurement baseline:** establish that the tasks, metrics, and controls mean what we think they mean.
2. **Level 1 — scaffolded persistence:** repeatedly invoke existing open models with an event stream, memory, self-state, goals, a clock, and autonomous update cycles.
3. **Level 2 — genuine latent recurrence:** carry a hidden state directly across time, including null-observation intervals, and intervene on that state causally.
4. **Level 3 — developmental organism:** train a small system whose native unit of computation has always been `(state, observation) → (new state, prediction/action)`, rather than bolting persistence onto a mature language model.

- persistence may add nothing beyond memory;
- quiet-time updates may collapse to noise or a fixed point;
- recurrent state may improve task performance without improving self-monitoring;
- self-report may improve without privileged access;
- endogenous regulation may stabilize identity but reduce flexibility;
- a developmental self-model may fail to emerge unless directly supervised;
- or some combination may generate a qualitatively new and mechanistically inspectable system.

A negative result narrows the design space. A positive result must survive controls before it receives a stronger interpretation.
